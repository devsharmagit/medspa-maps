## What are dermal fillers?

**Dermal fillers are injectable gels placed just beneath the skin to restore lost volume, soften lines and folds, and subtly enhance facial contours. The most common type is made from hyaluronic acid, a sugar molecule your body produces naturally to keep skin hydrated and plump.**

As we age, we gradually lose collagen, fat, and even bone in the face. That loss is what makes cheeks look flatter, under-eye hollows deeper, and folds around the mouth more pronounced. Rather than freezing the muscles that cause expression lines, which is what Botox and other neuromodulators do, fillers physically replace the volume that time has taken away, or add volume where you want more definition.

Because different formulations have different textures and properties, providers choose specific products for specific jobs: a thin, soft filler for delicate under-eye skin, a firmer, more structured one to rebuild a jawline or cheekbone.

> “Hyaluronic acid fillers are the only major filler type that can be dissolved. If you don’t love the result, a provider can inject an enzyme called hyaluronidase to reverse it.”

## What are the different types of dermal fillers?

**There are five main categories of dermal filler. They differ in what they’re made of, what they’re best at, how long they last, and whether they can be reversed.**

| Type | Common brands | What it does | Typical longevity | Reversible? |
|----|----|----|----|----|
| Hyaluronic acid (HA) | Juvéderm, Restylane, RHA, Belotero | Adds volume, smooths lines, hydrates | 6–18 months | Yes |
| Calcium hydroxylapatite | Radiesse | Restores volume, stimulates collagen | 12–18 months | Not easily |
| Poly-L-lactic acid | Sculptra | Gradually rebuilds collagen | Up to 2+ years | No |
| PMMA microspheres | Bellafill | Semi-permanent structural support | 5+ years | No |
| Fat transfer | Your own fat | Restores volume with your own tissue | Variable / long | N/A |

HA fillers are by far the most popular because they’re versatile, predictable, and reversible. Calcium hydroxylapatite and poly-L-lactic acid are often called “biostimulators” because they do more than fill: they prompt your skin to build new collagen over time, so results build gradually and can last longer. PMMA is the most durable and is considered semi-permanent, which is why it’s used selectively and only by experienced injectors.

## What do fillers treat, and where on the face?

**Fillers are used across the face and hands to restore volume, smooth folds, and refine shape. The most common treatment areas are the lips, cheeks, under-eyes, and the folds around the mouth.**

- **Lips:** adding volume, definition, and hydration, or balancing shape.
- **Cheeks and midface:** restoring lift and fullness that flattens with age.
- **Under-eyes (tear troughs):** softening hollows and shadows; a delicate area that needs an experienced injector.
- **Nasolabial folds and marionette lines:** the creases running from the nose to the mouth and down toward the chin.
- **Jawline and chin:** sharpening definition and improving profile balance.
- **Hands:** replacing lost volume so tendons and veins are less prominent.
- **Nose (“liquid rhinoplasty”):** camouflaging small bumps or asymmetries without surgery.

## How long do dermal fillers last?

**Most hyaluronic acid fillers last between 6 and 18 months, while collagen-stimulating fillers can last two years or more. How long your results last depends on the product, the area treated, and your own metabolism.**

Areas that move a lot, like the lips, tend to metabolize filler faster than more stable areas like the cheeks. Everyone’s body breaks filler down at a slightly different rate, so two people can get the same product and see it fade on different timelines. Your provider can recommend a maintenance schedule based on how your results hold up.

## How much do dermal fillers cost?

**Dermal fillers are usually priced per syringe, and many treatment areas require one to two syringes, while collagen-stimulating fillers are sometimes sold as a package of sessions. The total varies widely, so a consultation is the best way to get an accurate quote for your goals.**

Several things drive the cost: the specific product, how many syringes you need, your provider’s training and experience, and where you live. It’s worth remembering that filler is a medical treatment where skill matters a great deal, so the cheapest option isn’t always the safest or the most natural-looking.

## Are dermal fillers safe? Risks and side effects

**Dermal fillers are FDA-cleared and generally well tolerated when injected by a trained, licensed medical provider. Most side effects are mild and temporary, though rare, serious complications are possible, which is exactly why provider expertise matters so much.**

Common, short-lived side effects include swelling, bruising, redness, and tenderness at the injection site, usually resolving within a few days. Rare but serious risks include vascular occlusion, where filler blocks or presses on a blood vessel, as well as infection, lumps or nodules, and allergic reactions. A provider with deep knowledge of facial anatomy, and hyaluronidase on hand to reverse HA filler if needed, dramatically reduces these risks.

Results and candidacy vary from person to person, and no reputable provider can guarantee an outcome. A thorough consultation is how you and your provider decide whether fillers are right for you.

## Dermal fillers vs. Botox: what’s the difference?

**Fillers add volume to fill in lines and restore fullness, while Botox and other neuromodulators relax the muscles that create expression wrinkles. They solve different problems, and many people use them together.**

A simple way to remember it: fillers are for volume and shape (hollow cheeks, thin lips, deep folds), while neuromodulators are for movement-related lines (frown lines, forehead creases, crow’s feet). A provider can help you decide which, or both, fits your goals.

## What to expect: before, during, and aftercare

**A filler appointment is quick, often 15 to 45 minutes, with little to no downtime. Most people return to normal activities the same day, with minor swelling or bruising that fades within a few days.**

### Before

You’ll start with a consultation to discuss your goals, review your medical history, and map out a plan. To reduce bruising, providers often suggest avoiding alcohol, and blood-thinning medications or supplements when medically appropriate, for a few days beforehand.

### During

Your provider cleanses the area and usually applies a numbing cream; many fillers also contain lidocaine. The filler is injected with a fine needle or cannula, and your provider may gently massage the area to shape the result.

### Aftercare

- Avoid strenuous exercise for about 24–48 hours.
- Skip alcohol for the first day to limit swelling and bruising.
- Avoid touching or massaging the area unless your provider instructs you to.
- Sleep with your head slightly elevated the first night if you’re prone to swelling.
- Ask your provider about arnica or cool compresses if bruising appears.

## How to choose a filler provider

**Look for a licensed medical professional, a physician, physician assistant, nurse practitioner, or a registered nurse working under medical supervision, with specific training in injectables, a portfolio of natural-looking before-and-after photos, and a consultation-first approach.**

Ask who will actually perform the injections, what product they recommend and why, and how they handle complications. A provider who takes time to understand your goals and sets realistic expectations is worth more than a bargain price. A consultation can confirm whether you’re a good candidate and which approach fits your face.
